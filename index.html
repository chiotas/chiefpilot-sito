Same sim, same planner. Now there’s an airline.

Microsoft Flight Simulator gives you the aircraft, the world and a planner to prepare every flight. Chief Pilot keeps all of it, and adds what happens around the flight.

Before you take off: a mission worth flying, generated where you parked last night. While you fly: a second screen beside the TV. After you land: a receipt, a boarding pass and a new trail on the map.

And over all of it, the part the sim leaves to your imagination: an airline. Its fleet, its bases, its money, its reputation, and you, working your way up to captain.

See how one evening goes

Just one more flight.

An evening with Chief Pilot has four steps. Trouble is, the fourth leads back to the first.

  1. Pick a mission from the board.

    Work leaves from the airport where you last landed, sized to the aircraft you chose and to the legs you like.

  2. Plan it where you always plan.

    Make the flight plan in the MSFS planner, as usual. Chief Pilot reads your saved plans, finds this one and links it to the mission.

  3. Fly it in the sim.

    In the sim, EFB › Import Route. On the Mac, Off-block: the clock starts, with the route and the load sheet in view.

  4. Land, and get paid.

    Tap Landed, say how it went, read the receipt. The next missions leave from right here.

Where you are, what you fly

  • Missions start where you last landed.
  • They fit the aircraft you chose: seats, payload, range, runway.
  • And the legs you like: short, medium, long or oceanic.
  • Only the 28,388 airports that exist in MSFS.
  • Live METAR at both ends. Rough weather pays a bonus.

Every kind of job

  • Scheduled passengers and cargo.
  • Charters, weekend getaways, island hopping.

Your kind of airline

  • Tour: always onward, A to B to C, with a goal if you want one.
  • Scheduled airline: duties that start and end at base.
  • Charter & islands: short hops to holiday places.

  • Change it whenever you like. The board follows.

Build where you fly.

Buy an area and choose a hub in it. Add airports. On each base, build. Every structure has three levels, and an area around it where missions pay more.

  1. Level 1 +10 % on its missions within 100 NM.

  2. Level 2 +20 % within 200 NM, and fuller cabins.

  3. Level 3 +30 % within 300 NM. Where areas overlap, the best one counts.

What you can build

Every base opens with a standard terminal. Most structures you add put their kind of work on the board, wherever you fly, and pay more when those missions leave or land inside their area.

  • Passengers. Passenger terminal, VIP lounge, holiday charter desk.
  • Cargo. Cargo terminal, cold store, express courier.
  • Rescue. Medevac unit, firefighting and patrol base.
  • General aviation. Flying school, scenic flights, skydiving, banner towing, air taxi, island mail.
  • Services. Maintenance hangar, fuel depot, crew lodge, night lighting: cheaper flights from the base.

Flying back to one of your bases costs nothing. Running them costs money on the days you fly, and a structure in the wrong place doesn’t pay for itself.

Buy the areas where you want to work

Chief Pilot splits the world into 15 areas. Your hub’s area comes with the airline. You buy the others, and a bigger area, or one farther from your hub, costs more.

Each one comes with a hub of your choice and a free level 1 passenger or cargo terminal. Missions still leave from wherever you land. You can open bases only inside your areas, tours and regional contracts start only there, and rare missions turn up there more often.

Questions

Which versions of the sim does it work with? Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 on PS5, Xbox, PC and Mac. Chief Pilot never connects to the sim, so the platform makes no difference. Chief Pilot itself runs on your Mac.

Do I have to install anything in the sim? No. No add-ons, no SimConnect, nothing in the Community folder. You fly as you do now.

What does it read from the MSFS planner? The flight plans you’ve saved there. You sign in inside the app with the account you use for the planner, and Chief Pilot reads your plans. It never creates, changes or deletes one.

Does it make flight plans? No. It tells you where to go, with what and carrying what. You make the plan, in the planner.

How does it know how I landed? It doesn’t: you tell it. Butter, firm or hard, and the rate if you noted it. The clock runs from off-block, and the progress bar is an estimate on time.

Which aircraft can I use? Any you fly in the sim. Pick the type from the catalogue or build it by hand, then give it a registration and a name.

Where is my data? On your Mac. No account with us, no servers of ours, no tracking. Export everything as CSV, KML or a JSON backup whenever you like.

Does it work offline? The logbook and the career do. Fresh weather and new plans need a connection.
